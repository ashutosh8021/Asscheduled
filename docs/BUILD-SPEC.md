WEBSITE MASTER BUILD SPECIFICATION
Nude Project-inspired architecture × AS SCHEDULED travel experience model
1. NON-NEGOTIABLE CREATIVE DIRECTION
The website must be built with Nude Project as the structural and visual reference. Do not interpret this as a generic luxury travel website. The goal is to reproduce the reference site's editorial commerce architecture, pacing, navigation logic, card treatment, oversized visual storytelling, section hierarchy and interaction feel, while replacing fashion/product content with curated trips, experiences and events.
Reference: Nude Project website architecture and visual rhythm.
Business model: AS SCHEDULED / curated travel and experience platform.
The website must feel like a brand people want to belong to, not a conventional travel agency.
Keep the design bold, editorial, youthful, premium and slightly irreverent.
Use real brand assets, approved photography and approved copy in the final implementation.
Do not copy proprietary text, photographs, logos or source code from Nude Project. Recreate the design principles and information architecture for the new brand.

3. GLOBAL HEADER AND NAVIGATION
Announcement bar at the very top.
Primary header with brand/logo on the left.
Main navigation: TRIPS, EXPERIENCES, STORIES, EVENTS, CLUB.
Right-side utilities: SEARCH, ACCOUNT, MY TRIP.
Header must work as a premium editorial navigation, not a conventional travel-agency menu.
Desktop header should remain visually minimal and highly legible.
Mobile should collapse into a clean menu while preserving the brand logo and primary action.
Use hover/active states and subtle transitions.
MY TRIP should lead to the user's trip/application/account area.
4. HOMEPAGE: EXACT STRUCTURAL SEQUENCE
Build the homepage in this order unless the final approved reference requires a minor spacing adjustment:

5. HERO
Use a full-screen or dominant visual hero inspired by Nude Project's large-image editorial treatment.
One decisive headline, not a wall of copy.
Short supporting statement.
Primary action that moves users into discovery or application.
Use immersive photography/video with strong subject framing.
Typography should be large enough to function as part of the visual composition.
Hero should feel like a campaign, not a booking portal.
On mobile, preserve the visual impact rather than simply shrinking the desktop composition.
6. UPCOMING
Section title: UPCOMING.
Show the next experiences with strong visual cards.
Each card should communicate destination, experience name, timing/status and relevant price or starting point.
Use a horizontally scrollable editorial treatment where appropriate.
Cards should have strong image-first hierarchy.
Clicking a card opens the corresponding experience page.
7. MOST WANTED
Section title: MOST WANTED.
Feature experiences with the strongest demand, cultural relevance or desirability.
Use larger hero-style cards for priority experiences.
Create scarcity/desire through editorial presentation rather than fake urgency.
Show real availability indicators where applicable.
8. ALMOST FULL
Section title: ALMOST FULL.
Feature experiences approaching capacity.
Use genuine availability data only.
The visual treatment should create urgency while remaining premium.
CTA should lead directly to the relevant experience/application flow.
9. TRIPS DIRECTORY
Dedicated TRIPS page.
Editorial grid/list of all available trips.
Filters should be simple and useful: destination, type, date/status and other approved categories.
Cards should remain image-led.
Do not turn the directory into an e-commerce spreadsheet.
Every trip card must have a clear route to its detail page.
10. EXPERIENCES PAGE
Dedicated EXPERIENCES page for experiences that may exist independently from full trips.
Use the same editorial card system as TRIPS.
Separate trip-level information from individual experiences where the business model requires it.
Keep discovery visually consistent across the site.
11. STORIES
Dedicated STORIES section.
Editorial storytelling should make the brand feel culturally relevant and lived-in.
Use large photography, short headlines and concise editorial summaries.
Stories can cover trips, people, destinations, moments, behind-the-scenes content and community.
Story pages should prioritize reading and visual rhythm rather than conversion clutter.
12. EVENTS
Dedicated EVENTS section.
Show upcoming events and event-led experiences.
Each event should have date, location, title, visual, status and CTA where applicable.
Events should feel like part of the same cultural universe as the trips.
13. CLUB
Dedicated CLUB destination.
Position CLUB around belonging, community and access.
Do not make this look like a generic loyalty program.
The visual treatment should reinforce scarcity, identity and community.

Large campaign-quality visual.

Destination.

Availability/status.
Primary CTA.

16. TRIP SUMMARY
Duration.
Destination(s).
Departure point.
Date(s).
Group size or capacity where relevant.
Starting price.
Key experience category.
Any essential eligibility information.
17. TRIP STORY
This section is emotional, editorial and visual. It explains why the trip exists and why someone should want to experience it. It should not read like a generic tourism description.
Strong opening statement.
Short editorial paragraphs.
Supporting imagery.
Specific moments and access.
Distinctive character of the trip.
Clear sense of what makes the experience different.
18. WHAT YOU'RE ACTUALLY GETTING
Make the purchase tangible.
Explain exactly what the guest receives.
Accommodation, when included.
Transport/transfers, when included.
Experiences and activities.
Hosts/guides/coordinators, when included.
Meals, where included.
Access, reservations or special arrangements, where included.
Physical/digital materials, where included.
Explicit exclusions where a reasonable customer could otherwise be confused.
19. THE SCHEDULE
Use an expandable itinerary.
Each day/phase gets a clear title.
Show destination, activity, movement, stay and important timing.
Keep the default state compact.
Opening an item reveals the full detail.
The component must work exceptionally well on mobile.
20. WHAT'S INCLUDED / NOT INCLUDED
Two clearly distinguishable lists.
Use concise labels.
Avoid burying important conditions in prose.
Keep the visual design consistent with the rest of the experience page.
21. ACCOMMODATION AND TRANSPORT
Show accommodation category/property only where approved.
Show room/package logic where relevant.
Explain transportation and transfers clearly.
Use imagery where it adds meaningful value.
Do not invent hotel names, transport providers or service levels.
22. PACKAGE SELECTOR
This is a core conversion component.
Display every approved package/tier.
Show price for each.
Show the material differences in inclusions.
Allow immediate selection.
Clearly mark the recommended option if one exists.
Show add-ons separately.
The selected package must remain visible as the user progresses toward application/payment.
Mobile selector must be simple enough to operate with one hand.
23. APPLICATION FLOW
The application is intentionally part of the product. The site should not behave like a normal instant-book travel marketplace.
Profile: name, Instagram, phone/email, age, gender, occupation, city/college.
Trip details.
6 to 7 wild vibe questions.
Review application.
Pay ₹500 non-refundable application fee.
The ₹500 is credited against the final trip payment.
Selection decision.
If selected, complete remaining payment.
Get scheduled.
Travel.
The application UI should feel exclusive but not hostile.
Explain that selection is part of the experience.
Keep the application visually consistent with the brand site.
Use a clear progress indicator.
Save progress where technically appropriate.
Validate all required fields.
24. REVIEWS / SOCIAL PROOF
Use real testimonials/reviews only.
Prioritize short, vivid statements.
Where appropriate, connect reviews to specific trips.
Do not make the section look like a generic review widget.
25. WHO THIS IS FOR
Explain the intended audience and social energy of the experience.
This is especially important because selection is based partly on vibe/community fit.
Keep the writing confident and concise.
Avoid discriminatory or exclusionary language.
26. FAQ
Cover practical questions that could block application/payment.
Eligibility.
Application process.
₹500 fee.
Selection.
Payment.
Cancellation/refund policy.
Accommodation.
Transport.
What to bring.
Safety/coordination.
Other trip-specific questions.
27. FINAL CTA
Repeat the main action at the bottom of the page.
CTA should clearly communicate APPLY / BOOK / JOIN depending on the approved trip state.
Do not introduce a new visual language at the end.
28. VISUAL SYSTEM
Nude Project-inspired editorial commerce rhythm.
Large imagery.
Strong typography.
High contrast where appropriate.
Generous negative space.
Asymmetry where it improves the composition.
Minimal but distinctive UI.
Cards should feel editorial rather than dashboard-like.
Micro-interactions should be fast and subtle.
Avoid generic luxury clichés.
Avoid excessive gradients, glassmorphism and over-rounded SaaS components.
29. MOTION AND INTERACTION
Smooth page transitions.
Image hover behavior on desktop.
Subtle text/CTA motion.
Accordion animation for THE SCHEDULE.
Package selector should update instantly.
Mobile interactions must be touch-first.
Do not use animation that delays access to content.
30. MOBILE
Treat mobile as a designed experience, not a compressed desktop site.
Compact header/menu.
Full-bleed or carefully cropped hero media.
Horizontal card rails where appropriate.
Readable itinerary accordions.
Sticky or persistent primary CTA only where it improves conversion.
Package selection must remain extremely clear.
No horizontal overflow.
31. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
Build reusable components for header, cards, hero, itinerary accordion, package selector, FAQ, CTA and footer.
Keep trip content/data separate from UI components.
Create one reusable experience-page template.
Use dynamic data for dates, pricing, capacity and package information.
Prepare account/application state for MY TRIP.
Prepare analytics events for discovery, card clicks, applications, fee payment, package selection and final conversion.
Optimize all images and media.
Use semantic HTML, accessible controls and proper heading hierarchy.
Implement SEO metadata and social sharing metadata.
Test desktop, tablet and mobile before deployment.

33. DEFINITION OF DONE
A visitor must be able to enter through the homepage, immediately understand the brand, discover a desirable trip, open the experience, understand the story, know exactly what they are getting, inspect the schedule, compare packages, apply, pay the ₹500 application fee and understand what happens next. The website should feel like a cultural/lifestyle brand with travel at its core, not a travel portal wearing expensive clothes.
34. FINAL INSTRUCTION TO THE BUILD AGENT
Use Nude Project as the primary structural reference. Reproduce the quality of hierarchy, editorial pacing, visual scale, navigation simplicity, image-led merchandising, card systems and interaction polish. Then translate that system into the AS SCHEDULED model: curated trips, experiences, stories, events, club, application-first selection, ₹500 non-refundable fee credited against the final trip payment, selection, payment, scheduling and travel. The result should be recognizable as the same class of digital experience while remaining an original brand implementation.
